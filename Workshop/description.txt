[h1]Impatient Commuters[/h1]

Makes waiting passengers more likely to leave an overcrowded stop and find another route. Probability is shaped by two research-backed behavioural effects (balking on arrival and frustration over time) and is modified by age, trip purpose, line frequency, and whether alternative stops are nearby.

[h1]What it does[/h1]

The vanilla game already has a boredom system: passengers who wait too long eventually give up. But there is no crowd awareness. A passenger standing alone at an empty stop and one packed into a crowd of fifty behave identically.

This mod adds crowd-based impatience on top of the vanilla system. When a stop has [b]more waiting passengers than the average capacity of the vehicles serving it[/b], each waiting citizen gains a small per-step chance of giving up. Two distinct behavioural effects combine:

[list]
[*][b]Balking[/b] — a citizen who [i]just arrived[/i] at a heavily overcrowded stop is likely to turn around immediately. This probability decays as time passes: the longer someone has already waited, the less likely they are to abandon (sunk-cost effect). Supported by queuing theory (Haight 1959) and transit studies (Liu et al. 2022).
[*][b]Frustration[/b] — a slower quadratic curve that builds with accumulated wait time. Represents the classical boredom and irritation of a long wait at a crowded stop.
[/list]

[h1]Modifiers[/h1]

[h2]Age[/h2]

Each behavioural component is scaled separately by age group. Balk factors are fixed (research-backed); frustration factors are fully configurable in settings.

[list]
[*][b]Balk — Seniors ×1.4, Teens ×1.2[/b]: seniors have the highest crowding sensitivity (physical discomfort); teenagers are the most likely to check their phone and reroute immediately.
[*][b]Balk — Children ×0.5[/b]: children have low agency and rarely make independent rerouting decisions.
[*][b]Frustration — configurable per group[/b]: default range from Patient (×0.7 for children) to Impatient (×1.3 for seniors). Five preset levels from Very Patient (×0.5) to Very Impatient (×1.6).
[/list]

[h2]Trip purpose[/h2]

When enabled, balk and frustration are scaled in [b]opposite directions[/b] by where the citizen is going — reflecting the real difference between knowing you have alternatives vs. knowing you must arrive.

[list]
[*][b]Going to work[/b] — high balk (×1.2, commuters plan alternatives), low frustration (×0.4, must arrive).
[*][b]Going to school[/b] — moderate balk (×0.8), low frustration (×0.5).
[*][b]Going home[/b] — mild balk (×0.9), moderate frustration (×0.7).
[*][b]Leisure / shopping[/b] — neutral balk (×1.0), slightly higher frustration (×1.1).
[*][b]Tourists[/b] — low balk (×0.7, don't know alternatives), high frustration (×1.3, no fixed schedule).
[/list]

[h2]Line frequency[/h2]

Headway depends on both the number of vehicles [b]and[/b] the length of the line. A single bus covering 3 stops completes its circuit quickly; the same bus on a 100-stop line barely comes back. The mod uses stop count as a proxy for circuit length: [i]factor = √(stops ÷ vehicles ÷ 5)[/i]. A metro with 6 stops and 4 trains (×0.55) keeps passengers far more patient than a single bus on a long cross-city route (×2.0 or higher). Maximum cap ×3.0, minimum floor ×0.30.

[h2]Alternative lines nearby[/h2]

If another transport line has a stop within 150 m, every citizen at the overcrowded stop gets a [b]×1.15 bonus[/b] on their total probability. Knowing a viable alternative is close by makes leaving significantly more appealing.

[h1]Capacity threshold[/h1]

The mod only activates when [b]passenger count ≥ average vehicle capacity × Threshold Multiplier[/b]. A busy minibus line with 12-seat vehicles triggers at much lower crowd levels than a metro line with 180-seat trains. If no vehicles are deployed yet, the threshold is infinite — the mod has no effect until vehicles are on the road.

[h1]Settings[/h1]

Open [b]Options → Impatient Commuters[/b]:

[list]
[*][b]Enable mod[/b] — master on/off switch.
[*][b]Capacity threshold[/b] — multiplier on average vehicle capacity (0.5×–2.0×). Raise it to only trigger at very full stops; lower it to trigger sooner.
[*][b]Scale by trip purpose[/b] — toggle the destination-based balk and frustration factors.
[*][b]Patience per age group[/b] — five preset levels per age group for the frustration component.
[*][b]Debug logging[/b] — logs each forced departure to the game log with stop ID, crowd count, and probability.
[/list]
